How it works

Glowing particles are placed on a 3D math shape. Switch shapes (buttons or keys 1–5) and the cloud morphs into the next one, done on the GPU.

Equations

  • z = f(x,y) — surface · y = f(x) — 2D graph · r = f(θ) — polar
  • [X(u,v), Y(u,v), Z(u,v)] — parametric
  • f(x,y,z) = 0 — implicit surface (e.g. x^2+y^2+z^2-4 = 0)
  • d/dt = [fx, fy, fz] or dx/dt = fx, dy/dt = fy, dz/dt = fz — ODE trajectory
  • slice4d F(x,y,z,w) = 0; w=0.5 — a 3D slice of a 4D surface
  • complex re, im, outRe, outIm; w=0 — 2D flow field in the plane z = w
  • Separate several equations with ; to cycle through them
  • Include a standalone t to animate over time
  • Use a b c k with the param sliders to explore a whole family
  • Paste a second equation into 2nd eq to render two shapes at once
  • fourier square / sawtooth / triangle — a real partial-sum synthesis that keeps adding harmonics and resets; add a term count (fourier square 6) to freeze it
  • Vec Field draws the real gradient of a z=f(x,y) equation as colored arrows

Available: ^ pi e sin cos tan asin acos atan atan2 sqrt pow exp log abs min max floor ceil round sign.

Controls

  • Drag to orbit · scroll to zoom · click & hold to attract particles
  • Pause freezes a morph — scrub it back and forth with the Morph slider
  • Queue shapes, then Loop blends through them forever
  • Camera adds cinematic auto-orbits; Grid/Glints/Trails are display layers
  • X/Y/Z mirror the cloud across a plane; Curl swirls mid-morph; Shade adds a soft falloff; DoF is bokeh depth blur
  • Surprise me jumps to a random shape; Auto keeps exploring
  • If the frame rate drops, particle count and sparkle layers scale down automatically
